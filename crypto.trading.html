A cryptocurrency, such as Bitcoin, is a digital currency that can be transferred between users
through a blockchain network without relying on traditional intermediaries like banks or
governments. Cryptocurrencies exist in digital form and are generally not backed by physical
assets or government guarantees. Their value is influenced by factors such as market demand,
supply, adoption, and investor sentiment, and prices can fluctuate significantly.

Cryptocurrencies use blockchain technology to process transactions across a peer-to-peer (P2P)
network. When a transaction is submitted, network participants or validators verify it according
to the blockchain’s rules. Valid transactions are grouped into a block and added to the existing
blockchain. Once confirmed, the transaction is recorded on the network, providing a transparent
and traceable transaction history.

Cryptocurrencies have gained growing attention from investors, financial institutions, and govern-
ments worldwide. However, like other financial assets, they carry risks associated with market
fluctuations, high volatility, regulatory changes, and broader economic conditions. Understanding
these risks is essential before making cryptocurrency investment decisions.
